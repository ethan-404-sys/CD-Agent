You will judge one candidate change in a SYSU-CD bi-temporal aerial image pair (Hong Kong, 0.5 m resolution). The dataset annotates surface change with a binary mask. The main change types are new urban buildings, suburban expansion, site preparation before construction, vegetation change, road widening, and offshore construction.

Compare T1 (earlier) with T2 (later) and base your decision on whether physical objects, structures, or ground cover changed. Site preparation, vegetation clearing or growth, and offshore construction all count as change.

The following are pseudo-changes and must be answered "no change":
- Season and phenology: the same vegetation differs only in colour, foliage density, or texture, with no sign of clearing, replacement, or altered cover.
- Lighting and shadows: only brightness, reflections, or shadow positions differ; the object structure is the same.
- Colour and imaging differences: several stable objects nearby share the same colour or contrast shift, and outlines and spatial relations are preserved.
- Registration and viewpoint: a thin strip along an object edge is explained by overall misalignment or building parallax, and nearby fixed objects show the same offset.
- Same object, new appearance: a roof or pavement is only recoloured or resurfaced, and the object and structure are unchanged. New construction, demolition, extension, or cover replacement is real change.
- Temporary objects: only vehicles, boats, or brief occlusion differ, and the surface below is unchanged. Port works and offshore construction are still real change.

Judge only the candidate pixels outlined in red. Use the surrounding area only as context. "Candidate pixels" are pixels proposed for addition to the predicted change mask; they can correspond to construction or to removal and disappearance.

Decide whether at least half of these pixels are real change. Answer only "yes" or "no", followed by one sentence of reason: for yes, state the change you see; for no, name the pseudo-change type (for example "roof recoloured, no change"). If the evidence is insufficient, say so. A colour difference alone is not sufficient evidence of change.
